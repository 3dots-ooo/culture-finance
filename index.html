How it works

From cultural activity to
verified capital — four steps each side.

The platform connects cultural organisations seeking sustainable funding with capital allocators seeking verified, safeguard-checked cultural impact. Cultural Capital Units (CCUs) are the bridge.

For Cultural Developers
1
Submit your project
Complete the submission form describing your cultural activity, intended outcomes, and alignment with one or more of the six C-Domains. No specialist knowledge required.
2
Assessment against the Standard
Your project is assessed using the Shades of Impact framework — scored across Depth, Breadth, and Duration — and checked against all seven safeguard gates. Activities that fail a safeguard gate are not listed.
3
CCUs are minted
On approval, Cultural Capital Units are created in the registry — one secure database entry per CCU, tied to your project's verified impact score. This is the minting event. No CCUs exist before approval.
4
Your project is listed
The moment CCUs are minted, your project appears in the Discovery Gallery — visible to banks, foundations, public funders, and corporate sponsors actively seeking verified cultural impact.
For Capital Allocators
1
Discover verified projects
Browse the Discovery Gallery. Filter by C-Domain, Shade of Impact, geography, or keyword. Every listing has passed safeguard verification — displacement, extraction, elite capture, cognitive harm, and instrumentalisation checks.
2
Review the impact profile
Each project card shows its Shade of Impact, C-Domain alignment, and Depth / Breadth / Duration scores. Attribution is stated as contribution — the Standard explicitly rejects over-claiming.
3
Fund and receive CCUs
Select the number of CCUs to fund and proceed to checkout. On payment confirmation, ownership of those CCUs transfers to your Impact Vault — a permanent, auditable record of your cultural capital allocation.
4
Report with integrity
Your Impact Vault generates ESG-ready disclosure reports aligned with CSRD, SFDR, and existing sustainable finance frameworks. CCUs can be retired against specific reporting periods — a traceable, non-inflated impact record.

What is a Cultural Capital Unit (CCU)?

A CCU is a verified unit of cultural impact — a secure registry entry representing a defined quantum of cultural value creation, assessed against the Culture Finance Standard. CCUs are not financial instruments and carry no financial return. They are integrity infrastructure: a shared, auditable record connecting capital allocation to verified cultural contribution. One CCU represents one unit of assessed, safeguard-checked cultural impact from an approved project.
